Grandview Galleria: Torsional Integrity

An Interactive Mechanical Engineering Escape Room

You are a Year 2 structural and mechanical inspection intern trapped inside Grandview Galleria, a massive abandoned shopping mall slated for demolition.

A sudden grid surge has triggered the emergency blast shutters, sealing every exit. To restore auxiliary power and unlock the main exit, you must inspect the mall's decaying mechanical drive shafts and verify their torsional integrity before restarting the system.

Mission Briefing

How to Escape Grandview Galleria

Your objective is to analyse torsional shear stress, angle of twist, and shaft sizing criteria across three mechanical zones to safely restore power.

1. Three-Room Structure

Room 1 (Atrium Escalator Pit) and Room 2 (Service Corridor & Freight Elevator) are open immediately and can be completed in either order. Room 3 (Rooftop HVAC Control Room) is locked at the start.

2. Timer & Life Points

You have 20 minutes and 100 Life Points. Each incorrect answer deducts 10 Life Points. If time expires or Life Points reach zero, the security lockdown becomes permanent.

3. Document Reconstruction

Completing Room 1 reveals Document Fragment 1; completing Room 2 reveals Document Fragment 2. Combine both fragments on the Navigation Map to uncover the 4-digit keypad code for Room 3.

4. Hints & Feedback

Optional hints are available on every challenge without penalty. All questions provide immediate engineering feedback to guide your calculations.

Room 1 of 3

The Atrium Escalator Pit

Beneath the shattered glass dome of the central atrium lies the exposed drive assembly of the main escalator. The motor nameplate is intact, and the solid steel drive shaft shows signs of surface wear. You must analyze the torsional shear stress and twist to determine if the shaft can safely spin up without yielding.

Learning Objective: Analyse torsional shear stress and angle of twist in a solid circular shaft under applied torque.
Question 1.1 • Short Text Answer

The escalator drive motor delivers 15 kW of power at a constant rotational speed of 120 rpm. The solid circular steel shaft has a diameter of 40 mm. Calculate the maximum torsional shear stress (τmax) developed in the shaft in MPa (round to one decimal place).

MPa
Hint: First convert rotational speed to rad/s (ω = 2πN / 60) and find torque T = P / ω. Then apply τmax = 16T / (πd³).
Question 1.2 • Multiple Selection

To prevent the escalator steps from jamming against the comb plate, the angle of twist (φ) must remain small. Which of the following modifications would reduce the angle of twist for the same applied torque? (Select all that apply)

Hint: Examine the angle of twist equation φ = TL / (JG). To reduce φ, you must either decrease the numerator (T, L) or increase the denominator (J, G).

Room 2 of 3

The Service Corridor & Freight Elevator

Down a flickering service hallway, you find the mechanical room for the heavy-duty freight elevator. The drive assembly uses a stepped shaft with a keyway and a hollow transmission tube. Before energizing this circuit, you must evaluate stress concentrations, hollow shaft efficiency, and torsional failure modes.

Learning Objective: Compare torsional behavior in solid vs. hollow shafts, account for stress concentrations, and identify ductile vs. brittle torsional failure modes.
Question 2.1 • Concept Matching

Match each shaft design feature or material condition on the left with its correct mechanical consequence under pure torsion on the right.

Hint: Under pure torsion, maximum shear stress acts on transverse cross-sections, while maximum tensile normal stress acts at 45° to the shaft axis. Brittle materials are weak in tension; ductile materials yield in shear.
Question 2.2 • Dropdown Completion

Complete the inspection report regarding the hollow transmission tube (Do = 60 mm, Di = 40 mm) compared to a solid shaft of outer diameter 60 mm:

“In a circular shaft under pure torsion, shear stress varies from zero at the center to a maximum at the outer surface. Because the central core carries very little stress, removing the inner 40 mm core reduces the shaft's weight by , while reducing its torsional strength (maximum torque capacity) by only .”
Hint: Weight scales with cross-sectional area ((Di / Do)²), while polar moment of inertia J scales with the fourth power of diameter ((Di / Do)⁴).

Room 3 of 3 • Final Challenge

The Rooftop HVAC Control Room

You reach the rooftop mechanical penthouse. The main exhaust turbine shaft sheared during the storm, and you must select a replacement solid steel shaft from the emergency spare rack to safely power the ventilation and exit hydraulics without yielding or excessive twist.

Learning Objective: Design (size) a mechanical shaft against both strength (yield) and stiffness (angle of twist) failure criteria.
Question 3.1 • Design Sequencing

Arrange the engineering design steps in the correct logical order (1 to 4) to size a solid circular shaft subjected to both a maximum allowable shear stress (τallow) and a maximum allowable angle of twist (φallow). Use the Up/Down buttons to reorder.

Hint: You must find the applied torque before calculating required diameters, and you cannot pick a single final stock diameter until both strength and stiffness minimums have been computed.
Question 3.2 • Multiple Choice

The replacement solid steel shaft (L = 2.0 m, G = 80 GPa, τyield = 240 MPa) must transmit a torque of T = 2.5 kN·m. Design specifications require a Factor of Safety of 2.0 against yielding and a maximum angle of twist not exceeding 1.5°. Which minimum standard shaft diameter from the spare rack satisfies both requirements?

Hint: Remember to convert 1.5° to radians (1.5 × π / 180) and divide τyield by the Factor of Safety (2.0) to get τallow = 120 MPa.
